## Priority Issues

### [P1] Features section is structurally redundant
**What:** `features-section.tsx` sits between the app cards and the about section, repeating information the visitor just processed. "PawPress powers this site" is interesting but the card already says this. "One account, one credit balance" was already in the hero subtext.

**Why it matters:** On a persuade surface, every pixel must advance the decision or build trust. This section does neither — it echoes the cards above. A visitor who scrolls past the app cards has already seen both apps described.

**Fix:** Either replace with a "How credits work" explainer that adds genuinely new information, or remove entirely and let the page flow Hero → Apps → About → CTA.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] Hero headline is conceptually strong but semantically unclear
**What:** "My playground, Your workplace" (`hero-section.tsx:7-9`) is memorable but doesn't tell the visitor what the page offers. A first-time visitor must read the subtext to understand this is an app collection.

**Why it matters:** In a persuade context, the first 3 seconds determine whether the visitor stays. The headline must answer "What is this?" within that window. The subtext is long and the headline works as personality but fails as value proposition.

**Fix:** Consider restructuring: eyebrow = "Practical software by Sean," H1 = clear value proposition, subtext = personality line as secondary.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Credits mentioned 4 times without concrete explanation
**What:** Credits appear in hero subtext (`hero-section.tsx:13-14`), features section (`config/apps.ts:37-40`), navbar (`navbar.tsx:39`), and closing CTA (`closing-cta.tsx:8`). The visitor never learns what credits cost or what they unlock in these specific apps.

**Why it matters:** Repeating an unexplained concept four times creates anxiety. The visitor thinks "I need to understand this before I try an app" — which is friction on a page whose job is to get them to try an app.

**Fix:** Mention credits once in the hero subtext with the external link. Don't repeat in features or closing CTA.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P3] Stack tags are developer metadata, not user information
**What:** `app-card.tsx:31-33` displays technology tags (Next.js, Supabase, Tailwind). These serve no purpose for visitors seeking practical tools — they care about outcomes, not implementation.

**Why it matters:** The tags compete with the description for attention and make the card feel like a GitHub repo rather than a product.

**Fix:** Remove stack tags from cards. Surface technology in the app detail page or about section.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P3] No recommended or prioritized app
**What:** Both apps presented as equals in the grid (`apps-grid.tsx:14-16`). No signal to help the visitor choose which to try first.

**Why it matters:** Two equal options with no guidance creates mild choice paralysis. A "Start here" signal would reduce friction.

**Fix:** Add a subtle badge to one card or a one-line note: "New here? Try PawPress."

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

## Questions to Consider

1. **What if the hero showed a working tool instead of describing one?** A 10-second loop of PawPress publishing a page or Resumeow generating a PDF would be more persuasive than any headline.
2. **Is a full homepage the right format for a 2-app collection?** A single composed card for each app — no features section, no about section — might be more honest and effective.
3. **What if the about section came first?** "Hi, I'm Sean. I build practical tools." → then show the tools. Personal introduction builds trust before the ask.
